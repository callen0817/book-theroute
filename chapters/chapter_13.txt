Chapter 13 – Current: The Edge of the Path
The sun drops low, painting the sky in streaks of fire. Shadows stretch long across the uneven trail, roots reaching like fingers to trip the unwary. I keep my eyes on the ground, on the twists of the roots, on the subtle shifts in the soil. Ash walks beside me, lighter on his feet, scanning the edges of the path as if he could see the world differently than I do.
A figure waits where the path dips into a narrow valley. He is tall, lean, with a crooked smile that does not reach his eyes. He gestures toward a trail descending steeply. “This way,” he says. Words are half-understood by me, but Ash tilts his head, recognizing the accent and meaning.
I pause. Something feels off. My chest tightens, a quiet alarm deep inside. The knots, the water, the Archivist’s lines—they all taught me to observe beyond the obvious. Yet I hesitate. The path looks open. The figure’s posture is welcoming.
Ash glances at me. His eyes are questioning but confident. He steps forward slightly, then stops. He does not fully understand my pause, yet he senses it. I gesture subtly, pointing at the cliff edge just beyond the figure’s suggested trail. He stiffens, understanding dawning.
The Valley Feeder. The air shifts. The stranger’s smile falters. He knows that we have sensed something, even if we cannot fully name it.
We move carefully, keeping a distance. The valley is narrow, jagged rocks below, and a thin line of trail carved by past travelers. The stranger gestures again, urging, coaxing, a subtle menace hidden beneath civility.
I think of the knots I saw earlier. The boy, the Keeper, the twists marking paths. Some knots lie. Some paths lead to death. Observation is not enough; understanding intent matters.
Ash glances at me. I nod. We walk the edge slowly, testing the ground, aware of the weight in every step. The stranger follows, voice soft, friendly, but insistently guiding.
A sudden gust sends loose stones sliding. I catch a fallen stick, testing it as I would a tool, imagining the way a shift could topple us. Ash laughs quietly, the tension breaking slightly. Humor, even on the edge, keeps the mind sharp.
The path curves, and I notice a small cluster of twisted roots forming a subtle pattern, almost like a knot in hair. A hidden message? A warning left by someone who walked here before. I trace the pattern with my eyes, then nod to Ash. He sees it, and together we adjust our pace and angle.
The stranger’s patience wanes. His smile tightens. The lesson forms: not all who appear helpful truly are. Some will lead you to the edge, not out of malice alone, but because they do not know better, or because survival twisted their intent.
We emerge from the valley, the path widening again. The figure steps back, watching, evaluating. No attack, no words beyond polite caution. He allows us to pass.
I exhale slowly. Ash pats my shoulder, a silent acknowledgment. Trust is fragile. Observation alone does not suffice. You must read the world, the people, and your own instincts.
The path continues. Rocks, roots, water channels, subtle markings. Each step is a lesson. Each step is a test.
And somewhere, faint but present, the shadow of the Valley Feeders lingers, a reminder that not all guidance is safe, and not all who walk the path have the same understanding of right and wrong.

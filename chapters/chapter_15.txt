Chapter 15 – Current: The Hollowed Farm
The path narrows between two ridges, sunlight spilling in fractured patterns through the sparse trees. I feel the shift in air—heavier now, warmer, almost sweet, but under it a faint decay. Ash walks beside me, scanning the edges, alert, always alert.
Ahead, a structure rises, its shape familiar from earlier tales: low, rectangular, with smoke curling lazily from a broken chimney. The smell of cooked grain—or what I think is grain—carries faintly, masking a faint iron tang underneath.
We approach cautiously. Figures move within the structure, beckoning. “Food, shelter,” they call. “Help with the work and return stronger.” Their gestures are practiced, polished, persuasive. But my chest tightens. The instinct from the valley, from the near-fall, presses in.
Ash notices my hesitation and glances at me. His eyes are sharp. He understands instinct, though the words escape him. I nod, subtle, and we circle around, keeping the sun at our backs.
Inside, the workers—some young, some old—move quickly, efficiently. They greet travelers with smiles, hands extended. One child, thin and pale, pauses at the edge of a low wall, watching. Their eyes are wide, unblinking, and I feel the echo of fear beneath the surface.
We see the subtle signs: footprints veering off the main floor, small grooves along the walls, traces of paths that don’t belong. The smell grows stronger, unmistakable now. Ash steps forward, placing himself subtly between the child and the open ridge. I follow.
A faint tremor in my stomach, a whisper of what the valley had taught me. The Farm is hollowed—not as a place of provision, but as a trap. Volunteers, promised resources, safety, work—they disappear.
I glance at Ash. His eyes catch mine, a spark of understanding. The child moves slightly closer to us, curiosity mixing with caution. I see the fear behind their gestures, but also the trust—they are desperate for help, even as they don’t fully understand what help might mean.
I think of the twisted roots, the knots in hair, the Keeper’s warnings. Deception is precise, elegant, seductive. Yet observation, caution, and partnership unravel the design.
We guide the child along a safer path, out of the immediate reach of the workers, whose attention is divided, their gestures distracted by the tasks they continue to perform with mechanical precision. Ash smiles faintly, light flickering in his eyes. Humor, subtle, releases the tension just enough to maintain sharpness.
We leave the hollowed structure behind, but the scent lingers. Iron, earth, decay. The path stretches forward, open now, yet shadowed by what we’ve seen. I think of Ash’s future, of the companions we will meet, and of the lives we will touch—sometimes lost, sometimes saved.
And somewhere within that hollowed farm, the first hints of tragedy ripple. Not yet understood, not yet named, but the lessons are being laid: trust your instincts, discern intent, and remember that even helpful hands may hide consequences.
The sun sinks lower. The path winds forward, stones and roots marking every step, and I know the Valley Feeders’ shadow follows, silent, patient, waiting for the next misstep.

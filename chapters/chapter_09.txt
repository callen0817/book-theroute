Chapter 9 – Current: Shadows and Intent
The path twists again, sloping upward through a shallow valley. The sun is warmer now, golden, but it casts long shadows across the stone and root-strewn ground. Every shadow moves with the light, but some feel heavier, denser, like they carry intent.
Ash steps lightly, scanning the edges of the path. I keep my eyes on the shadows too, trying to feel what the path itself is saying. Water trickles along the roots at our feet, its soft murmur both a warning and a guide.
A group appears in the distance, their movement deliberate, coordinated. Their hair is twisted, but not like the Keeper’s knots—rougher, unruly, tangled in ways that speak of urgency or aggression. Their eyes are bright, watchful. They smile, but it does not reach their eyes.
Ash stops. I stop. The path narrows. My heart beats faster, not from fear, but from the awareness that intent matters more than appearance.
The group approaches, speaking quickly, their words foreign, jagged. Ash tilts his head, listening, pretending he understands. I do not. I only watch.
One of them gestures to a branch, then to the ground, then back at us. A mockery of guidance? A trap? I cannot tell, not yet. My mind races through every warning, every lesson from the Keeper and the Archivist. Observation first. Trust cautiously. Action measured.
I see a small movement—something glinting in the hand of one of the strangers. A tool. Not yet a weapon. But intent twists it. I shift slightly, signaling Ash. He follows, his expression careful now, watching, learning.
The group smiles again, gesturing toward a narrow cliff-edge path. “This way,” they say in a voice we do not understand.
I study the ground, the roots, the angle of the cliff. Something feels wrong, unnatural. The water trickles toward the edge, carrying a sharp smell. My instincts flare. Not everything is what it seems.
I step forward, slowing, showing them we are moving, but in no rush. Ash mirrors me, confusion and humor mixing as he notices I do not follow their lead blindly. The group shifts, impatience creeping in, but our caution is deliberate.
Then I gesture toward a safer path, one that curves away from the cliff, tracing a hidden trail I noticed earlier. Ash nods. One of the strangers curses, but we do not understand it. We follow the safer path, water glinting at our feet, shadows falling behind us.
The group hesitates, then follows in a chaotic scramble. Their intent is exposed. We are no longer just travelers; we are observers of consequence.
We reach a clearing, the valley opening, sunlight pouring in. Ash looks at me, a mixture of awe and amusement. I cannot smile yet—my mind races—but I feel a spark of satisfaction.
The Keeper’s lessons, the Archivist’s lines, the water, the knots, the shadows—they all guide us. Not blindly, not perfectly. But enough to walk safely, enough to understand that not everyone can be trusted, even if they appear helpful.
Ash finally laughs, low and soft, and I laugh too. Not at them, but at ourselves, at the moment of shared clarity. The shadows move behind us, but we carry our own light.

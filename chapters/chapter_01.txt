Chapter 1 – Embers: The First Steps
The air carries a chill, sharp enough to prick the skin beneath my sleeves. I feel the weight of my small body, the length of my legs unsure of themselves, carrying me forward over uneven ground. The path stretches before me, a narrow swath through wild grass, roots twisting underfoot, stones protruding like the skeleton of the earth. My parents’ hands, still warm from holding me, linger in memory, a press against my chest as they guided me here. They say nothing now. Their eyes are steady, familiar, and yet distant, like the ground itself.
A bird falls from the branch above, flutters, then lands on the dirt with a soft thud. Its wings are not ready; it flails. I pause. My heart tightens. The bird tries again, struggling, and then catches a gust, soaring briefly before plunging once more. My stomach twists. My father’s voice echoes in my memory: Walk your own path. You are ready.
I breathe. Step. Breathe. Step.
Every sound is new. Every movement is a question. The trees sway differently here. The air tastes like ash from a fire long extinguished. Or maybe it’s just the earth. I do not yet know.
A crackling somewhere to the left draws my gaze. Smoke curls from behind a fallen log. I approach carefully. A small fire, embers still hot, smolders in a shallow pit. Someone must have been here, someone I cannot see. I crouch and trace a stick through the dirt, drawing lines that feel like shapes but are not yet anything.
The path itself is quiet. I see it as a thread, winding through the landscape: forest, scrubland, a stream glittering like fractured glass. There are smells I do not recognize: flowers crushed underfoot, the sharp tang of water, the sourness of something fermenting. My legs ache. My stomach grumbles. Hunger feels like an echo I do not yet name.
I hear movement. Not voices, not words. Only the rustle of fabric, the scrape of boots against stone. My pulse hammers. My mind searches for what to do, what is expected. My parents have taught me some things—how to hold a tool, how to follow tracks, how to stay unseen—but no one has taught me how to recognize the intent of a shadow, or the quiet decision behind a footstep.
A man passes just beyond the bend. His hair is long, twisted and knotted into coils that loop over each other like the roots of the forest. I do not yet know the name for this. I only know it is unusual. He moves swiftly, silently, carrying a pack tied with leather strips, and disappears before I can speak. Something in me stirs—a mix of awe and curiosity. The world is larger than I thought.
I reach a clearing where the path bends, the sun brushing the treetops with gold. The dirt under my hands is warm. I crouch and let my fingers trace patterns in it. Lines. Knots. Twists. Shapes that might mean nothing to anyone but me. They are mine.
A gust of wind rattles the branches above. Leaves fall, scattering across the dirt like small flames. Fire. I feel it inside me, the spark that makes me pull my feet closer to the earth, that pushes me forward with some invisible tug. I do not yet understand it. Motivation. Intuition. Something in me recognizes its presence.
I look back. The forest behind me is shadowed. The memory of my parents is dimmer now, their hands a ghost pressing against my chest. I hear my own breath. Hear my own heartbeat. I step forward again.
The path is waiting.

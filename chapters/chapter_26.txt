Chapter 26 – Current: Shadows of Leadership
The path widens into a plain dotted with settlements, scattered smoke rising from simple homes. The air smells of sun-baked earth, fire, and the tang of dried meat. Life hums here, steady and deliberate, yet there is an undercurrent, a rhythm of fear and obedience that does not fit the landscape’s simplicity.
Ash walks ahead, relaxed, speaking softly to himself in his language, hands gesturing. I follow, listening, watching. We notice the people first by their movement, then by the subtle signs of hierarchy. Some carry knotted hair, others braided, their patterns intricate, conveying maps I cannot yet read.
We encounter a small group on the edge of the plain—uniformed, silent, their tools polished and sharp. Their presence feels deliberate, controlling. Villagers step aside with practiced fear. Ash slows. I feel a tightening in my chest: instinct, memory, intuition. Something about them is wrong.
A boy of our age, braided hair twisted like a map across his scalp, approaches us cautiously. His hands are raised, open, signaling peace. He speaks rapidly, words I understand only in fragments. The Keeper’s lessons echo: language is a bridge, but gestures are the truth. I nod, and he relaxes slightly.
“They call them Valley Feeders,” he says. “They take volunteers. They say it is for the farms, for the harvest. They say it is for the good of everyone.”
Ash’s eyes narrow. I sense his disbelief before he fully understands. “Do they…?” I ask softly, unsure how to voice the truth we both feel but cannot yet name.
The boy’s eyes are wide, ashamed. “Some…some do not return. Some…are used. It is for survival.”
We exchange a look. The lesson is immediate, brutal even in the hush of the plain: leadership does not equal righteousness. The rules, once designed to protect, have been twisted. Obedience becomes cruelty. Greed becomes a justification.
We walk past the settlement, careful, observing. Some of the Valley Feeders keep strict logs, others operate on rumor, fear, instinct. The people obey not because of love or loyalty, but because survival demands compliance.
Ash leans close, speaking softly in his tongue. I catch enough to understand: anger, frustration, disbelief—but tempered by comprehension. We are learning that the path requires more than endurance; it requires ethical judgment, courage to intervene, and wisdom to discern action from reaction.
The plain ends in cliffs, sharp drops into a dry riverbed. The shadows of the settlements stretch long and menacing, reminding us that the road carries consequences for choices we make, or fail to make.
We camp that night, fire small and careful. I study Ash’s face in the flickering light—he is thinking, learning, understanding, humbled. Our shared silence speaks volumes: we will not blindly follow instruction, and we will not fear making choices when the truth is clear.
Lulu watches from a distance, quiet, patient. I feel the weight of history in her eyes. She trusts us, though not blindly, because she knows that understanding is earned. We sleep with the path around us, the knowledge of corruption and consequence pressing like shadows.
In the morning, the sun ignites the plain, light catching in the braided hair of travelers already moving along the paths we have yet to walk. The lesson remains: the road does not forgive ignorance, and the choices of those in power shape life and death alike. But even in the shadows, there is the possibility of guidance, correction, and redemption.
As we prepare to leave, a small group of Keepers arrives from the southern ridges. Lulu watches them, her eyes reflecting recognition and a quiet longing. She turns to us, gestures conveying that her path now aligns with theirs. Ash and I understand immediately. We do not see it as ungratefulness. Everyone has their own path, and at times we are guided by forces more than what can be explained or expressed. We bid her farewell, knowing the road connects all who walk it. We rise, pack our few tools, and continue. The path ahead is unmarked, unyielding, but ours to walk, ours to navigate with care, and ours to shape with conscience.

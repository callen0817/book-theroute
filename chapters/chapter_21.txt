Chapter 21 – Current: False Paths
The path curves sharply, disappearing behind a ridge of jagged rocks. Shadows stretch long across the ground, and the wind whispers between the stones like voices that do not belong. Ash and I move cautiously, stepping over roots and loose pebbles, alert to every shift, every crack of branch or stone.
A traveler appears at the ridge. They are older, their hair twisted into knots that shimmer in the sun, their gait confident, commanding. They call out to us, voice warm, inviting, offering guidance.
I pause. My memory of the Keeper and the Archivist tugs at me. Knots can map truth, words can preserve it—but both can lie. The traveler smiles, gesturing to the ridge, to the path ahead. Their knots are unfamiliar, inconsistent with the rhythms I have learned. My gut tightens.
Ash leans forward, eyes narrowing, reading the posture, the gestures. I feel the unspoken: something is off. I step slightly ahead, signaling caution. The traveler laughs softly, as if reading our hesitation. They point to a side trail that drops steeply, nearly out of sight.
I remember the Archivist’s lesson: permanence does not guarantee truth. The Keeper’s map can be trusted—but only if observed carefully. I trace an invisible loop in the air, mimicking the pattern of their hair, their gestures. Something twists, falters. This is not a true path.
Ash catches my eye, understanding flickering between us without words. We take the main route, ignoring the side trail, hearts pounding. The traveler’s smile does not falter, but their gaze follows us, a shadow that stretches beyond the rocks.
We pause at the next clearing, breath heavy. The sun is lower, painting the trees in gold. I realize the lesson: not all who map, who speak, who guide are honest. Some lead to false paths. Some lead to cliffs. Some lead to ruin.
Ash shifts, pointing to a distant glimmer—a fire, perhaps a camp, flickering in the forest. It is not ours. It is not the Keeper’s. It is not the Archivist’s. We approach cautiously, eyes open, senses alert. The path is wider, the air heavier.
The traveler’s shadow lingers in my mind. I trace knots with my fingers in the air, matching their gestures with the patterns I know to be true. I see the divergence, the false loops. We move carefully, deliberately.
By the time we reach the camp, dusk has fallen. The fire burns small, controlled. Figures sit around it, their knots and papers promising guidance, wisdom, truth—but the rhythm of their gestures feels off, too smooth, too practiced. They watch us closely, calculating, assessing.
Ash and I exchange a glance, unspoken agreement passing between us. We listen, observe, learn—but we trust only what aligns with the lessons we carry. The Keeper. The Archivist. The path itself.
And as night falls, I realize: deception is part of the journey. Not all who guide are friends, not all maps are true. But even in false paths, there is learning. We move on, carrying knowledge, doubt, and caution as companions.
The fire burns low behind us. Ahead, the path winds into darkness, and we step forward, ready to meet the next lesson, the next traveler, the next twist in the road.

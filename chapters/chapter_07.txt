Chapter 7 – Current: Knots in the Flow
The path narrows again, trees closing overhead, leaves brushing our shoulders, roots twisting like fingers across the ground. Water runs quietly in the shadows, hiding in dips and pockets, moving as though it carries messages we are not yet able to read.
Ash moves first, silent, confident. I follow, focusing on the rhythm we’ve established, letting my eyes and ears stretch to match his.
I notice hair in the branches. Twisted, knotted, strange patterns woven into the strands. Someone has been here. Someone important. I cannot yet name it, but my skin pricks with recognition, the same way the water pricks against my ankles, alert and alive.
A woman steps from behind a tree. Her presence is calm, deliberate. Her hair is twisted, intricate, heavy with meaning. I recognize it instinctively, though I do not know why. She studies us, eyes sharp, body poised.
Ash pauses. I pause. The water pools around our feet, reflecting the dappled light above. I feel the weight of her gaze and the tension in the air.
She gestures to the ground, fingers tracing patterns in the dirt. I understand her message, though not her words. Follow. Observe. Learn.
We move carefully. She does not speak, but her actions are clear. She shows us paths through the roots, the stones, the small channels of water, each step deliberate. Her hands press to the earth, tracing symbols I do not yet know how to read. Twisted hair, knotted strands—maps, she tells me without words, maps of the paths, the secrets of movement, the lessons of observation.
Ash watches her closely, mimicking the motion of her hands. I try to follow, struggling to grasp the meaning, but I catch fragments—intertwined, twisted, deliberate. Knowledge hidden in hair, in roots, in movement.
She turns to me, tilts her head. I feel a thread of connection, unspoken understanding. She is from where I come from. I am the only one who understands, at least for now. The rest of the world has not reached this place, not yet.
We follow her deeper into the grove. Small pools of water catch our reflections. My own face stares back at me, eyes wide, alert. Ash mirrors my movements, silently, perfectly.
She pauses at a small clearing. Patterns etched in the dirt, twisted hair left on sticks, symbols pressed into the ground. I kneel, studying them. They tell stories of paths taken, of knowledge gathered, of mistakes made.
The woman steps back, watching us. I sense judgment, but also trust. She is offering what she knows, but not everything. I nod, silently acknowledging her teaching. Ash nods too.
Water flows at our feet, constant, unbroken. Twists and knots everywhere. I realize the path itself is a living thing, shaped by those who walk it, marked by those who know how to read it.
We move on, careful, attentive. Every step, every gesture, every glance matters. Observation, patience, trust—lessons embedded in movement and silence. The world is teaching, and we are learning.

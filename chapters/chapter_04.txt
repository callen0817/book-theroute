Chapter 4 – Embers: Crossing Shadows
The path narrows. Rocks jut from the earth, roots snake across the ground, and the air grows cooler, heavier. My legs burn with the climb, but I move steadily. I remember my parents’ words, their hands pressing my shoulders: Walk your path. Keep your eyes and mind open.
I hear a snap behind me, faint but distinct. I stop. Heart racing. A figure emerges between two trees: tall, lean, with hair tied in thick coils that sway like vines. Another traveler. Dreadlocks, I think. They carry a long tool, a staff of some kind. I do not yet know if it is meant to build, or break, or protect. My hands tighten around a fallen branch.
They watch me. I watch them. No words. Nothing. Just observation. They step aside, letting me pass. No harm, no guidance. Just a presence. The path allows this. The path teaches.
Further along, a clearing opens. Sunlight spills across soft grass. And there—I feel it before I see it—the familiar presence beside me. It is the boy, crouched low, observing a cluster of small rocks. His hair is loose, long, moving with the wind. He does not speak. His eyes catch mine, sharp and welcoming.
I stop. He stops. The distance between us closes with a shared understanding. I still do not know his name, but I know he is not a threat. My hands brush the earth, my stick relaxed, ready.
He gestures—slowly, deliberately. Points to the path ahead, then to himself, then to me. I follow his motions, tracing shapes in the dirt, marking a map without words. He nods once, small, and steps aside. I feel it then, a pull, a recognition, like a thread stretching from him to me. The path connects us.
The sun dips lower. Shadows stretch long across the clearing. I sense the movement of others. Footsteps on dry leaves. Voices distant, incomprehensible. The boy moves forward, glancing back at me once, then disappears into the forest.
I remain still, letting the quiet settle. A small fire glows in the distance, smoke curling upward. I approach carefully, feeling the warmth against the cooling air. The familiar boy. The path brought him here—though I do not yet know his name is Ash.
The fire crackles. Shadows twist across faces I cannot fully see. I crouch at the edge of the glow. The smell of smoke, of food, of the forest. The boy sits silently, hands brushing the earth, listening. Listening as I listen. I understand this. No words yet. Only observation. Only learning. Only connection.
I settle nearby, keeping the distance, letting the rhythm of the fire, of the path, guide me. I am not alone. And I am learning that presence can speak louder than words.

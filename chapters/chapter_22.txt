Chapter 22 – Current: The Valley Feeders
The path narrows as we descend into a rocky valley, walls rising like guardians, silent and stern. The sun falls low, casting long shadows that stretch across the ground, folding the valley into darkness.
Ash moves ahead, steps light but deliberate, scanning the edges where shadows cling. I follow, careful, tracing every subtle movement in the earth, every whisper of wind. My fingers twitch, recalling the patterns of the Keeper, the Archivist, the false paths of travelers who lie.
A sound cuts through the stillness: the scrape of a blade against stone. I freeze. Ash halts beside me. From the shadows, figures emerge—faces obscured, hair twisted into tight, jagged knots, their eyes cold and calculating. They move with purpose, directing others toward the edges, toward the cliffs.
The Valley Feeders. I know the stories now, warnings whispered on the path: they send people off cliffs, follow orders, manipulate, claim necessity. Volunteers, they say, but the truth is worse. Hunger, greed, power twisted into survival.
I grip the tool at my side, the one I have always thought of as a simple stick, a lever, a helper. I know now it can be more. But still, I hesitate. Ash stands firm, silent, ready. Together, we watch, calculating.
One of them steps closer, gesturing toward a narrow trail along the cliffside. Their voice is smooth, persuasive: come this way, they say, and find shelter, find food, find safety.
I remember the side trail from earlier, the false maps. I see the pattern, the twist in their knots. The cliff is real, the danger literal, and yet the deceit is in the smile, the invitation.
Ash glances at me. I nod. We move slowly, deliberately, staying in the shadow of the rocks, avoiding direct paths that could be traps. One misstep could send us down to the rocks below, swallowed by stone and silence.
We reach a boulder, pause. Breath comes sharp, hearts racing. The Valley Feeders spread out, unaware of our movement, confident in their control. We slip past, guided by instinct, memory, the unspoken lessons of observation and patience.
And then, from a hidden crevice, a faint cry—a figure, trapped, bound by ropes, eyes wide with fear. A female Keeper. Her hair knotted, patterns complex. She trusts us immediately, or perhaps she trusts the survival instinct she sees in us. I understand her words; Ash understands none, yet our intentions align. Together, we cut her free, careful not to alert the Valley Feeders.
The Valley Feeders notice only moments later, but by then we are gone, slipping back onto the path, carrying the freed Keeper with us. She whispers her name, Lulu, her gestures precise, conveying lessons, warnings, gratitude.
We move through the valley, darkness thick around us. I feel the weight of what we have seen: necessity and corruption, deception and greed, survival twisted into cruelty. The path continues, winding, uncertain, and I understand something new. Not all dangers are visible, not all lies are easy to recognize.
Ash walks beside me, silent but steady, his presence a reminder of friendship, trust, shared instincts. Lulu's hands brush mine briefly, a silent acknowledgment. Her knowledge, her map of the hidden paths, joins ours.
The valley recedes behind us. Ahead, the path climbs again, winding into shadow and light, and we step forward, carrying the lessons of the valley, the burden of knowledge, and the growing certainty that even in danger, we move together, learning, surviving, walking.

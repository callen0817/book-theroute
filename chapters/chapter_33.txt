Chapter 33 — When Children Walk
The valley does not measure time the way travelers do.
Years pass not in miles but in growing trees, worn paths, and children whose steps become longer with each season. What once felt like a place of fragile beginnings has become something steadier now, shaped by patience and care.
Ash’s children no longer run through the fields without purpose.
They move with awareness.
They notice the wind shifting through the grass. They pause to watch birds circling above the river. They ask fewer questions now, not because curiosity has faded, but because understanding has begun to take root.
I see Ash watching them often.
He does not interrupt their wandering. He lets them make small mistakes, lets them discover the subtle signs he once taught them to read. Sometimes he offers a quiet correction, a gentle reminder, but more often he simply observes.
They are learning to walk.
Not just with their feet, but with their minds.
One morning I find Ash standing at the edge of the forest while his children explore deeper among the trees. They move carefully, testing their footing across fallen logs, reading the ground the way he once showed them.
“You’ve taught them well,” I say.
Ash does not answer immediately.
He watches them the way a traveler studies a distant road, measuring something beyond what the eye can see.
“They’re ready to start asking their own questions,” he says quietly.
The wind moves through the branches above us.
I remember the moment when the road first began whispering to me. That quiet pull beyond the places I already understood. It never arrived loudly. It simply waited until curiosity grew stronger than comfort.
Ash’s children are beginning to feel it now.
In the weeks that follow, their wandering stretches farther from the valley.
They return with stories—strange animal tracks, unfamiliar streams, distant hills glimpsed through gaps in the forest. Their voices carry excitement, the same restless wonder that once pushed all of us beyond the places we thought were enough.
Ash listens to every word.
Sometimes he smiles. Sometimes he asks careful questions that guide them toward answers they have not yet considered.
But he never tells them to stay.
One evening the valley gathers near the river, fires burning low as the sky darkens above the hills. Ash sits quietly while his children talk among themselves, their voices carrying a new kind of energy.
Plans.
Possibilities.
Places they want to see.
I notice the moment when they finally speak the words aloud.
“We want to walk,” one of them says.
Not far, they explain. Just beyond the hills at first. Just enough to see what lies past the edges of the world they know.
Ash listens without interrupting.
The firelight flickers across his face, revealing the quiet weight of memory behind his eyes. For a moment I wonder if grief might hold him here, anchored by the life he built beside the river.
But Ash has always understood the road better than most.
He looks at his children with calm acceptance.
“You should,” he says simply.
They stare at him, surprised by how easily the answer comes.
“The world teaches things the valley never can,” he continues. “And the road has been waiting for you longer than you realize.”
They begin preparing the next morning.
The settlement gathers small supplies for them—simple tools, dried food, small maps drawn from memory and shared experience. Nothing elaborate. Walking has never required much beyond awareness and patience.
Ash walks with them to the ridge overlooking the valley.
From there the land opens outward in every direction, forests and hills stretching toward horizons none of us have fully explored.
His children pause at the edge of the path.
They look back once.
Ash stands steady behind them.
“You’ll find your way,” he says.
They nod, the excitement of the unknown shining clearly in their eyes.
Then they begin walking.
Ash remains on the ridge long after they disappear among the trees.
I join him there after some time.
“They’ll be fine,” I say.
“I know,” he replies.
The valley lies peaceful below us, smoke rising gently from evening fires, the river reflecting the fading light.
For the first time in many years, Ash has no one left to guide.
No one left who needs him to stay.
The road stretches quietly beyond the hills.
And this time, when it calls, there is nothing holding him back.
